Create a Character

Approaches

Approaches describe how you meet a problem and how capable you are when acting that way:

ApproachCovers
ForceOverpower, break, drive, and exert direct pressure.
FinesseAct through precision, timing, coordination, and careful control.
GritWithstand pain, fear, hardship, exhaustion, and attrition.
ReasonAnalyze, recall, diagnose, plan, and understand systems.
InstinctNotice, react, navigate uncertainty, and trust experience.
PresenceCommand attention, perform, intimidate, and establish authority.
HeartEmpathize, encourage, connect, and act from faith or conviction.
CunningDeceive, maneuver, exploit weaknesses, and arrange an unfair advantage.

Starting Approaches

Choose an array and assign one rating to each Approach:

ArrayRatings
Focused3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1
Versatile3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1

Record Approaches as ratings from 1–5, such as Force 3. The chosen Approach contributes that many d6s to an action.

Unranked Features

Write one adventuring Archetype and four Traits. They are evocative, performable truths rather than ratings.

A Feature may be a Trait, Background, Archetype, relationship, piece of equipment, spell, Condition, or another specific truth recorded on the character sheet. Choose only the single most applicable Feature for an action. It establishes identity, permission, and effect; its die size comes from position.

Character Texture

Define or generate a Background, inciting incident or motivation, Hearth Tie, Road Tie, Appearance, Keepsake, Mundane Item, Comfort, and Haven.

Texture establishes knowledge, access, permission, obligations, recovery, revelations, and consequences. When one of these truths is the most applicable Feature, it may ground an action.

Starting State

  • Fatigue 0 and Fatigue Floor 0.
  • No Wounds, Conditions, or XP.
  • Seven personal equipment slots plus a backpack granting three additional slots. The backpack's slots begin as Open Pack permissions.
  • A character with a magical Feature begins with three Tier 1 spells total.

Resolve Uncertain Action

  1. Establish the immediate fiction.
  2. The player states a goal and method.
  3. The GM states what is possible, what threatens the attempt, and likely consequences.
  4. Choose an Approach and a relevant Feature.
  5. Roll the pool, determine one result, change the fiction, and move the spotlight.

Action pool: a number of d6s equal to one Approach rating, one Feature die, and any additional dice from Push or Tag Team.

A relevant Feature normally contributes d8. Roll the pool and add the highest d6 to the Feature die. Additional d6s from Push and Tag Team join the same pool.

Static Difficulty

Every action has Difficulty 8. A total of 8 or higher succeeds; a lower total fails. Then read every d6 in the pool to determine the final result.

Totald6 PoolResult
Below 8Any 1Fumble. The action fails, the character gains 1 Fatigue, its ordinary consequence lands, and the character makes a Rally check.
Below 8No 1sFail. The action fails, the character gains 1 Fatigue, and its ordinary consequence lands.
8+No 6sSuccess at Cost. The action succeeds and an announced cost lands.
8+Exactly one 6Success. The action succeeds without the immediate cost.
8+Two or more 6sExceptional Success. The action succeeds and exceeds its original intent.

Every d6 counts 1s and 6s, including dice added by Push or Tag Team. The Feature die determines the total but never supplies an effect result. Every roll produces exactly one result. Added dice can improve the quality of a success but cannot make an already-successful action worse.

Advantage

When meaningful leverage, preparation, or favorable circumstances improve the action, the Feature contributes d12.

Disadvantage

When genuinely compromised or desperate circumstances hinder the action, the Feature contributes d4.

Advantage and Disadvantage cancel, returning the Feature to d8. If no Feature applies, the character may still attempt the action with a fallback d4. Advantage can raise that fallback to d8. Routine actions still happen without rolling, and impossible actions remain impossible.

Push

Before rolling, the acting character may pay 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s. The Fatigue is paid regardless of the result, and any result-generated Fatigue stacks afterward.

Tag Team

When characters commit to one coordinated action, state one shared goal and choose a lead. Each helper must describe a contribution that makes sense in the fiction and is supported by one of their Features. They may then pay 1 Fatigue and add one d6. The Fatigue is paid regardless of the result, and a helper contributes only once.

Roll once and resolve one shared outcome. The action's consequences may affect any helper whose contribution placed them in danger. Any number of characters may help when every contribution meets these requirements.

Success at Cost

The stated intent happens, but an announced cost lands. The cost may add Fatigue, reduce effect, consume time or equipment, attract danger, worsen position, or force a difficult choice. It cannot erase the success.

Failure

A Fail or Fumble normally adds 1 Fatigue to the character making the roll, on top of any Push cost. The GM may establish a higher amount before the roll when the pressure clearly warrants it.

After adding Fatigue, apply the action's ordinary consequence.

Fumble and Rally

When the total fails and any d6 in the pool shows a natural 1, make one Rally check after gaining failure Fatigue. Never make more than one Rally check from an action, regardless of how many 1s appear.

The Rally result is added to the action's ordinary consequence. Helpers do not make additional checks, though either consequence may affect a helper whose contribution placed them in danger. Successful rolls never trigger Rally from their dice; the established fiction may still require it independently.

Position and Consequences

Communicate position concretely: the ledge is crumbling, the crossbow is trained on you, the passage is too narrow for the axe, or the ritual will attract something. Reserve Disadvantage for genuinely compromised action; grant Advantage for meaningful leverage, preparation, or favorable circumstances.

Consequences can add Fatigue, inflict a Wound or Condition, damage or consume equipment, remove opportunity, cause delay, put someone in danger, change a relationship, or let an announced threat land. An established consequence does not require another roll.

Spotlight and Opposition

There is no separate combat mode and enemies do not roll. The GM announces threats; players describe how they answer. Move the spotlight after every resolved action so every positioned character can intervene. Use clockwise order only if free spotlight becomes chaotic.

Ordinary opposition may fall after one decisive action. Durable threats may require fictional breakpoints, vulnerabilities, or a short progress track.

Fatigue, Wounds, and Conditions

Fatigue and Rally

Fatigue is the toll of exertion, hunger, fear, weather, poor sleep, glancing harm, blood loss, magic, and deprivation. It ranges from 0–20 and can never recover below the current Fatigue Floor. The fiction may add Fatigue directly.

When a Fumble or reaching 20 Fatigue triggers Rally, resolve the triggering event and its ordinary consequences first. Then roll 1d20. Roll over current Fatigue to hold together. Roll equal to or under it and apply the same numbered Fallout below. Rally Fallout never clears Fatigue or retracts what the character accomplished.

d20Rally Fallout
1Adrenaline. The next action you attempt before the immediate situation changes has Advantage.
2Winded. You cannot Push or contribute to a Tag Team until you get a moment out of immediate danger to catch your breath.
3Jumpy. Gain 1 Fatigue; one nearby companion also gains 1 Fatigue.
4Overwhelmed. Your next action has Disadvantage, after which this effect ends.
5Dark-Shy. The first time in a scene that reliable light is lost or you enter darkness without it, gain 1 Fatigue.
6Blood-Shy. The first time in a scene that violence begins or someone nearby suffers a Wound, gain 1 Fatigue.
7Restless. A respite cannot reduce your Fatigue. A good camp or safe haven still can.
8Loss of Confidence. Attach a concrete Condition to the Approach used and reduce its current rating by 1.
9Afraid To Be Alone. The first time in a scene that you become separated from every companion, gain 1 Fatigue.
10Faltering. Whenever you Fail or Fumble an action, gain 2 Fatigue instead of the ordinary 1 Fatigue. Additional Fatigue from Push, magic, and other effects is unchanged.
11Road-Worn. Gain 1 Fatigue when beginning another dangerous route leg without having made a good camp since the last one.
12Nightmares. A good camp counts only as a respite for you unless another character uses their Connect camp activity with you. A safe haven still works normally.
13Magic-Sick. The first time in a scene that uncontrolled or hostile magic affects you or your surroundings, gain 1 Fatigue.
14Fixated. Name something from the triggering situation that you cannot let go. Attach the Condition to the Approach used and reduce its current rating by 1. The first time in a scene that you turn away from the fixation, gain 1 Fatigue.
15Collapse. After the triggering action, you cannot act for the rest of the scene unless another character gets you somewhere safe.
16Coming Apart. Attach a distinct concrete Condition to the Approach used and to one other Approach changed by the pressure. Reduce each current rating by 1.
17Spiraling. Until this Condition is resolved, make each future Rally check twice and use the worse result.
18Compounding Problems. Roll twice more on this table and apply both results. Reroll 18–20; duplicate persistent results escalate as described below.
19The Body Gives Out. The accumulated pressure becomes Wound-capable. Suffer a Wound, attach it to the Approach used, and reduce that Approach's current rating by 1.
20Meet Death. At maximum Fatigue, the accumulated exhaustion, exposure, fear, pain, or magic becomes fatal. Meet Death regardless of current Wounds.

Results 1–4 and 15 are immediate and may recur. Results 5–14, 16, and 17 create Conditions that remain until their fiction is meaningfully resolved; the listed rule is their mechanical effect, not a complete prescription of how they appear. If a persistent result is already active, use the next higher result that is not already active. Results that attach new Conditions to Approaches may recur when each Condition is concretely different.

Fatigue gained while resolving Rally Fallout does not immediately trigger another Rally, though a character at 20 Fatigue still cannot continue until they recover.

Approach Fallout

Every Wound and Condition that the table says to attach is written beneath one Approach and reduces its current rating by 1. The player chooses and names the affected Approach subject to what happened in the fiction; when a result specifies the Approach used, it must go there. Multiple tags can affect the same Approach or different ones, and their reductions stack. An Approach at 0 cannot be selected.

Clearing an attached Condition restores its reduction. Treating an attached Wound restores its reduction; healing it removes the Wound as usual. Wounds still count toward Meet Death and set the Fatigue Floor.

ApproachName the Fallout by asking
ForceWhat can you no longer sustain, move, or bring to bear?
FinesseWhat has begun shaking, slipping, or falling out of rhythm?
GritWhat can you no longer endure without giving ground?
ReasonWhat thought has clouded, trapped, or escaped you?
InstinctWhat danger do you now hesitate before or overreact to?
PresenceWho saw you falter, or what confidence cracked?
HeartWhat fear, grief, conviction, or attachment now controls you?
CunningWhat lie, tell, vulnerability, or shortcut has caught up with you?

Other Conditions

Conditions outside the Rally table remain unranked fictional states such as Poisoned, Lost, Hunted, Disguised, or Blessed Against Fire. They change permission, position, and consequences rather than applying a standard modifier unless a rule says to attach them to an Approach. A Condition clears when its fiction is resolved.

Meet Death

When a character with three Wounds would take another—or result 20 invokes Meet Death directly—they become Dying. Describe how Death and her threshold appear, then roll 1d10.

  • 10: Death returns you but permanently marks you. Retain three Wounds.
  • 6–9: Death returns you Maimed, indebted, transformed, bound, or permanently changed. Leave the immediate scene.
  • 1–5: Your time has come. Choose a final exceptional deed followed by death, extraordinary supernatural transformation or servitude, or willing passage into death.

Maimed

Meeting Death can leave a character Maimed. Record the permanent injury or loss concretely. A Maim changes fictional permissions, position, and consequences rather than applying a standard penalty. Adaptation, prosthetics, or powerful magic may change its effects without erasing what happened. If a Maim makes continued adventuring impossible, the character must retire.

Magic

Each spell has a fixed Tier from 1–5 and text defining effect, scale, reach, requirements, and permission. To cast it, know the spell, use a relevant magical Feature, and choose an Approach rating equal to or greater than the spell's Tier.

Casting uses the ordinary Difficulty 8 action roll and five results. It never adds a separate Arcane die.

  1. Start with a number of d6s equal to the chosen Approach rating.
  2. Remove one d6 for each spell Tier above 1.
  3. Add any Push or Tag Team dice.
  4. Add the magical Feature die, roll, and resolve the result normally.
Spell TierApproach Reduction
1None
2−1d6
3−2d6
4−3d6
5−4d6

Because the Approach rating must equal or exceed the spell's Tier, this always leaves at least 1d6 before Push or Tag Team dice are added.

ResultSpellcasting Outcome
Exceptional SuccessThe spell works with heightened effect.
SuccessThe spell works as intended.
Success at CostThe spell works, remains available, and the caster gains Fatigue equal to its Tier.
FailThe spell fails, becomes exhausted, and the ordinary consequence lands.
FumbleThe spell fails, becomes exhausted, the ordinary consequence lands, and a magical Mishap replaces the normal Rally check.

A Mishap is the magical interpretation of the ordinary Fumble result, not a separate trigger or check. Spell Tier removes more dice, causes more Fatigue on Success at Cost, and scales Mishap severity without changing Difficulty.

Tag Team still uses one caster and one spell. Helpers may add d6s after the Tier reduction through knowledge, equipment, positioning, ritual support, or other relevant Features. Casting a second spell is a separate action.

One Work activity at camp restores all exhausted spells when the caster performs their tradition's recovery practice. Learned spells are lost only through explicit fiction that severs their source.

Until a Mishap table exists, resolve a trigger as a severe magical consequence shaped by the spell, Tier, tradition, place, and immediate fiction. Record it for later design.

Advancement

Treasure and consequential discoveries grant Shadowdark-compatible XP when returned to safety. Every participating character receives the full XP value.

End of Session

Each party question answered yes grants every participating character 1 XP:

  • Did we reach somewhere new or learn something consequential about the wider world or its history?
  • Did we survive or overcome a dangerous threat through violent or nonviolent means?
  • Did we leave a person, place, or community better off at meaningful cost?

Each character question answered yes grants that character 1 XP:

  • Did you reinforce, struggle against, or meaningfully change who your character is when it mattered?
  • Did your relationship with or opinion of someone or somewhere change through fellowship, conflict, comfort, or recovery?

Spend XP

Spend XP only during a quiet stretch in a safe haven and only when play supports the growth.
