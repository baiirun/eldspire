Create a Character

Approaches

Approaches describe how you meet a problem and how capable you are when acting that way:

ApproachCovers
ForceOverpower, break, drive, and exert direct pressure.
FinesseAct through precision, timing, coordination, and careful control.
GritWithstand pain, fear, hardship, exhaustion, and attrition.
ReasonAnalyze, recall, diagnose, plan, and understand systems.
InstinctNotice, react, navigate uncertainty, and trust experience.
PresenceCommand attention, perform, intimidate, and establish authority.
HeartEmpathize, encourage, connect, and act from faith or conviction.
CunningDeceive, maneuver, exploit weaknesses, and arrange an unfair advantage.

Record Approaches directly as dice, such as Force d8. They advance through d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12.

Unranked Features

Write one adventuring Archetype and four Traits. They are evocative, performable truths rather than ratings.

A Feature may be a Trait, Background, Archetype, relationship, piece of equipment, spell, Condition, or another specific truth recorded on the character sheet. Choose only the single most applicable Feature for an action. It establishes identity, permission, and effect; its die size comes from position.

Character Texture

Define or generate a Background, inciting incident or motivation, Hearth Tie, Road Tie, Appearance, Keepsake, Mundane Item, Comfort, and Haven.

Texture establishes knowledge, access, permission, obligations, recovery, revelations, and consequences. When one of these truths is the most applicable Feature, it may ground an action.

Starting State

  • Fatigue 0 and Fatigue Floor 0.
  • No Wounds, Conditions, or XP.
  • Seven personal equipment slots plus a backpack granting three additional slots. The backpack's slots begin as Open Pack permissions.
  • A character with a magical Feature begins with three Tier 1 spells total.

Resolve Uncertain Action

  1. Establish the immediate fiction.
  2. The player states a goal and method.
  3. The GM states what is possible, what threatens the attempt, and likely consequences.
  4. Choose an Approach and a relevant Feature.
  5. Roll the pool, keep the highest two dice, add them, apply the result, and move the spotlight.

Action pool: one relevant Approach die, one Feature die, and any additional dice.

A relevant Feature normally contributes d8.

Static Results

Every action uses the same result bands. Add the highest two dice in the pool and compare their total below.

Highest-Two TotalResult
2–6Failure. Danger lands or the situation worsens.
7–10Success with consequence. Achieve the intent with a cost.
11–14Clean success. Achieve the intent and avoid the immediate threat.
15+Exceptional success. Exceed the original intent.

Advantage

When meaningful leverage, preparation, or favorable circumstances improve the action, the Feature contributes d12.

Disadvantage

When genuinely compromised or desperate circumstances hinder the action, the Feature contributes d4.

Advantage and Disadvantage cancel, returning the Feature to d8. If no Feature applies, use a fallback d4. Advantage can raise that fallback to d8. Routine actions still happen without rolling, and impossible actions remain impossible.

Tag Team

When characters commit to one coordinated action, state one shared goal and choose a lead. Each helper must describe a contribution that makes sense in the fiction and is supported by one of their Features. They may then pay 1 Fatigue and add exactly one die: either a relevant Approach or their Feature's d8. A helper contributes only once.

Roll once and resolve one shared outcome. The lead alone gains ordinary failure Fatigue; helpers have already paid to participate. The action's consequence or Rally fallout may affect a helper when their contribution places them in danger.

Push

Before rolling, the acting character may pay 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s. The Fatigue is paid regardless of the result, and any result-generated Fatigue stacks afterward.

Push buys reliability through personal exertion; Tag Team brings another character's relevant experience or capability but may put that helper in danger. Any number of characters may help when each contribution meets those requirements.

Failure

A failure normally adds 1 Fatigue to the character making the roll, on top of any Push cost. The GM may establish a higher amount before the roll when the pressure clearly warrants it.

After adding Fatigue, apply the action's ordinary consequence.

Rally

Dice can trigger Rally only when an action roll fails. After a failed roll, if any die in the pool shows a natural 1, the character makes one Rally check after gaining failure Fatigue. Count every die in the pool, whether or not it was kept.

A successful roll never triggers Rally from its dice. The established fiction may still require Rally independently, such as when Fatigue reaches 20 or an explicit threat or rule calls for it.

Never make more than one Rally check from an action. Helpers do not make additional checks. Failed Rally adds one result from the Rally fallout table to the action's ordinary consequence; either may affect a helper whose contribution placed them in danger.

Position and Consequences

Communicate position concretely: the ledge is crumbling, the crossbow is trained on you, the passage is too narrow for the axe, or the ritual will attract something. Reserve disadvantage for genuinely compromised action; grant advantage for meaningful leverage, preparation, or favorable circumstances.

Consequences can add Fatigue, inflict a Wound or Condition, damage or consume equipment, remove opportunity, cause delay, put someone in danger, change a relationship, or let an announced threat land. An established consequence does not require another roll.

Spotlight and Opposition

There is no separate combat mode and enemies do not roll. The GM announces threats; players describe how they answer. Move the spotlight after every resolved action so every positioned character can intervene. Use clockwise order only if free spotlight becomes chaotic.

Ordinary opposition may fall after one decisive action. Durable threats may require fictional breakpoints, vulnerabilities, or a short progress track.

Magic

Each spell has a fixed Tier from 1–5 and text defining effect, scale, reach, requirements, and permission. To cast it, know the spell, use a relevant magical Feature, and use an Approach whose step rank equals or exceeds the spell's Tier: d4 is Tier 1 through d12 at Tier 5.

  1. Build the action pool normally from the relevant Approach, the magical Feature after applying Advantage or Disadvantage, and any additional dice.
  2. Roll one separate Arcane d8 for the spell.
  3. Keep the highest two action dice and read the action result. The Arcane die never contributes to this total.
  4. Read the Arcane die for exhaustion and a possible mishap.
  • The Arcane die exhausts the spell when it rolls equal to or under the spell's Tier, regardless of the action result.
  • A full action failure adds ordinary failure Fatigue and exhausts the spell.
  • If the action fails and the Arcane die shows 1, resolve one magical mishap instead of Rally. The mishap is added to the ordinary action consequence.
  • Otherwise, a failed action with a natural 1 among its action dice follows the normal Rally procedure. The Arcane die is separate and never triggers Rally.
  • An Arcane 1 on a successful action exhausts the spell but causes neither mishap nor Rally.

Tag Team still uses one caster and one Arcane die. Helpers may contribute through knowledge, equipment, positioning, ritual support, or other non-casting Features. Casting a second spell is a separate action.

One Work activity at camp restores all exhausted spells when the caster performs their tradition's recovery practice. Learned spells are lost only through explicit fiction that severs their source.

Until a mishap table exists, resolve a trigger as a severe magical consequence shaped by the spell, Tier, tradition, place, and immediate fiction. Record it for later design.

Advancement

Treasure and consequential discoveries grant Shadowdark-compatible XP when returned to safety. Every participating character receives the full XP value.

End of Session

Each party question answered yes grants every participating character 1 XP:

  • Did we reach somewhere new or learn something consequential about the wider world or its history?
  • Did we survive or overcome a dangerous threat through violent or nonviolent means?
  • Did we leave a person, place, or community better off at meaningful cost?

Each character question answered yes grants that character 1 XP:

  • Did you reinforce, struggle against, or meaningfully change who your character is when it mattered?
  • Did your relationship with or opinion of someone or somewhere change through fellowship, conflict, comfort, or recovery?

Spend XP

Spend XP only during a quiet stretch in a safe haven and only when play supports the growth.
