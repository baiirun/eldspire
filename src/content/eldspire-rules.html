Eldspire Expedition Rules

How To Play

A fantasy adventure game about dangerous roads, hungry ruins, tired heroes, and the warmth that makes returning worth the risk.

These are the current rules. Experimental alternatives are available as the step-dice variant and d6-pool variant.

Core Principles

  • Begin with the imagined world, not the character sheet.
  • Players state what they want and how they pursue it.
  • The GM describes what is possible, what is dangerous, and what could happen.
  • Roll only when the outcome is both uncertain and consequential.
  • Consequences follow from established fiction rather than a universal damage procedure.
  • The same procedure resolves every uncertain, consequential action.

The character sheet records evidence about who a character is. It is not a menu limiting what they may attempt.

Create a Character

  • Versatile: one Action at 3, five at 2, and the remaining seven at 1.

Background, Archetype, and Features

Choose or generate one Background, one Archetype, and four Traits. Background and Archetype combine without restriction. Together they establish identity, history, capability, permission, items, relationships, troubles, and questions.

Record one concrete Feature from the Background, one from the Archetype, and one from each Trait. Assign two as Expert and the remaining four as Practiced. Rate the specific playable fact—Monster Anatomy d10, Never Lost d8, or Courtly Education d8—rather than treating a broad packet label such as Hunter or Noble as a universal bonus.

Other character facts remain mechanically important even when unrated: they establish knowledge, access, permission, obligations, effect, consequences, and what the character notices. Features do not limit what a character may attempt.

Starting State

  • Fatigue 0 and Fatigue Floor 0.
  • No Wounds, Conditions, or XP.
  • Seven personal equipment slots plus a backpack granting three additional slots. The backpack's slots begin as Open Pack permissions.
  • A character whose Background, Archetype, or Traits grant magical permission begins with three Tier 1 spells total.

Resolve Uncertain Action

  1. Establish the immediate fiction.
  2. The player states a goal and method.
  3. The GM states what is possible, what threatens the attempt, and likely consequences.
  4. If the outcome is uncertain and consequential, choose the Action matching the concrete act and invoke at most one relevant Feature.
  5. Roll, apply the result, change the fiction, and keep or move the spotlight according to the new openings and urgent threats.

Roll d6s equal to the Action rating. A relevant Feature replaces one Action d6 with its d8, d10, or d12; it does not add another die. Read the highest die.

DiceResult
Highest is 1–4Failure. The action fails and the established consequence lands.
Highest is 5Mixed success. The action succeeds and an established consequence or cost lands.
Highest is 6+Clean success. The action succeeds without the immediate consequence.
Two or more dice show 6+Exceptional success. The action succeeds with greater effect or another fitting benefit.

Faces above 6 do not create additional result bands. Invoke a Feature for free when the player can name a concrete, credible connection. Accept that connection without grading its relevance; reject it only when the established fiction clearly does not support it. Multiple Features never stack.

At Action 0, roll two dice and keep the lower. Without a Feature, roll 2d6. With a relevant Feature, roll 2d8, 2d10, or 2d12 according to its rating. Only the kept die counts, so the roll cannot produce an exceptional success.

Help

When characters commit to one coordinated action, state one shared goal and choose a lead. Each helper must contribute concretely in the fiction, gain 1 Fatigue, and add one d6 to the lead's pool. Roll once. Everyone placed in danger shares exposure to the announced consequences.

Push

Before rolling, gain 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s. The Fatigue is gained regardless of the result, and any other Fatigue stacks afterward. Push cannot make an impossible action possible or bypass a fictional restriction.

Adjudicating the Fiction

There is no Situation die, dynamic Difficulty, or formal position-and-effect procedure. The Action and optional Feature determine the roll. Opposition, preparation, equipment, and circumstances determine permission, likely effect, consequences, armor, and how much progress is required.

Most adjudication should be a silent ruling. Bring it into conversation when a limitation or danger would not be obvious, the possible effect is uncertain, or different actions would materially change the situation. Consider four levers:

  • Permission: can the action work at all?
  • Position: what leverage or danger shapes the attempt?
  • Effect: what will success accomplish given method, scale, quality, and opposition?
  • Consequence: what established danger lands on a failure or mixed success?

Backgrounds, Archetypes, Traits, items, relationships, spells, preparation, and the environment give concrete reasons to pull those levers. Do not worsen position or reduce effect merely because an Action rating is low; the rating already changes the probability. Usually one dominant fictional factor is enough.

Consequences can add Fatigue, inflict a Wound or Condition, damage or consume equipment, remove opportunity, cause delay or exposure, change a relationship, or let an announced threat land. An established consequence does not require another roll and cannot erase a successful result.

Spotlight and Opposition

There is no separate combat mode and enemies do not roll. Conflict normally begins with a character's goal and method; the GM states what opposes the action and its likely consequences. Opposition acts first only when surprise, position, an earlier consequence, hesitation, or ignored danger gives it the lead.

Keep the spotlight where an action is still flowing. Move it when another positioned character can exploit an opening, answer an urgent threat, or has not participated recently. Do not introduce a new threat merely because a roll was resolved. Use clockwise order only if free spotlight becomes chaotic.

Ordinary opposition may fall after one decisive action. Durable threats may require fictional breakpoints, vulnerabilities, or a short progress track.

How Actions and Threats Work

Enemies do not take turns or roll dice. Conflict normally begins with what a character wants to change, not with an enemy attack that demands a response.

Goal First

The player states their goal and method. The GM describes what is possible, how opposition obstructs the action, what it threatens, and the likely consequences. Roll only when the exchange is uncertain and consequential.

One roll resolves both the character's action and any opposition involved. The threat is defined relative to the character's chosen goal; it does not automatically replace that goal with a defensive reaction.

When Opposition Leads

Opposition acts first only when the established fiction gives it the lead: surprise or ambush, prepared or overwhelming position, a previous consequence or failure, hesitation, or ignored danger.

When there is still time to intervene, give a Warning: name what is about to happen, what it threatens, and any important constraints. Any positioned character may answer. If the characters knowingly ignore the Warning, or a consequence is already established, it can land without another roll.

Resolve Both Sides

Read the roll against the character's intent and the established opposition:

  • Without a consequence, the character achieves their intent, overcomes the immediate opposition, and normally retains the lead.
  • With a consequence, the character achieves their intent but an established danger or related cost lands. The changed fiction determines who has the lead.
  • On a failure, the character does not achieve their intent; opposition acts, its threatened consequence lands, or it takes the lead.

A consequence on a successful roll cannot erase the success.

Lead and Spotlight

The lead is a fictional state, not a turn order or tracked resource. Keep the spotlight on a character while their action flows naturally. Move it to someone who can exploit an opening, answer an urgent threat, or has not participated recently.

No character is entitled to act before an established threat lands. The GM likewise does not need to introduce a new threat merely because a roll was resolved. Follow the changed fiction rather than alternating automatically between character actions and enemy attacks.

Example

Mara wants to cut off the pursuing ogre by collapsing a cracked arch. The GM says she can reach the weakened support, but the ogre will be on her before she finishes; a consequence could leave her trapped with it. One roll resolves both Mara's plan and the ogre's advance.

  • Exceptional success: the arch buries the ogre and leaves a safe route onward.
  • Clean success: the arch falls between the party and the ogre; Mara retains the lead.
  • Mixed success: the arch blocks the ogre, but Mara is stranded on its side of the collapse.
  • Failure: the ogre reaches Mara first and sweeps her away from the support, taking the lead.

The normal rhythm: goal → opposition and stakes → one roll → changed fiction. Use Warning → response only when opposition already has the lead.

Fatigue, Wounds, and Conditions

Fatigue and Fallout

Fatigue is a visible clock from 0–10 measuring exertion, hunger, fear, weather, poor sleep, glancing harm, blood loss, magic, and deprivation. It can never recover below the current Fatigue Floor.

  • Push: gain 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s.
  • Help: a helper who contributes a die gains 1 Fatigue.
  • Failure: after a consequential roll fails, the acting character gains 1 Fatigue.
  • Fictional pressure: established exertion, exposure, fear, violence, deprivation, or similar pressure can inflict 1–3 Fatigue: meaningful, severe, or overwhelming.
  • Explicit costs: spells, techniques, items, and other rules may list Fatigue as their price.

These sources stack. Announce voluntary costs and threatened Fatigue before commitment when possible. Because failure already adds 1 Fatigue, do not also use Fatigue as its ordinary consequence unless a distinct source of pressure justifies it.

When Fatigue reaches or exceeds 10, finish resolving the triggering action and its ordinary consequences. The character then suffers automatic Fallout; there is no Rally check. Name one Condition supported by the pressure that filled the clock. If a cause-specific Fallout table exists, the group may roll on it to determine the form of Fallout, never whether Fallout occurs.

After Fallout, clear 10 Fatigue while retaining any excess and respecting the Fatigue Floor. Reaching the threshold does not itself create a Wound or undo what the character accomplished.

Wounds

Wounds are lasting injuries caused only by Wound-capable fiction, such as Broken Ribs, Concussion, Burned Hand, Gashed Thigh, or Crushed Knee. Name each one concretely. Fatigue never automatically becomes a Wound.

Treating a Wound stabilizes it and changes its restrictions. Use the treated floor only when every current Wound is treated. Healing removes a Wound and follows the injury's fiction.

A Wound can be avoided or redirected only through concrete protection such as armor, a shield, sacrificed equipment, an ally, magic, retreat, or established positioning.

Conditions

Conditions are unranked fictional states such as Poisoned, Terrified, Lost, Hunted, Inspired, Disguised, Trusted by the Watch, or Blessed Against Fire. They change permission, position, and consequences rather than applying standard bonuses or penalties. A Condition clears when its fiction is resolved.

Meet Death

When a character with three Wounds would take another, they become Dying and Meet Death. Describe how Death and her threshold appear, then roll 1d10.

  • 10: Death returns you but permanently marks you. Retain three Wounds.
  • 6-9: Death returns you Maimed, indebted, transformed, bound, or permanently changed. Leave the immediate scene.
  • 1-5: Your time has come. Choose a final exceptional deed followed by death, extraordinary supernatural transformation or servitude, or willing passage into death.

Maimed

Meeting Death can leave a character Maimed. Record the permanent injury or loss concretely, such as Lost Eye, Severed Hand, Ruined Leg, Destroyed Voice, or Body Withered by Death. A Maim changes fictional permissions, position, and consequences rather than applying a standard penalty. Adaptation, prosthetics, or powerful magic may change its effects without erasing what happened. If a Maim makes continued adventuring impossible, the character must retire.

Recovery

Recovery requires safety, shelter, food, warmth, time, and appropriate care.

  • A meaningful moment of respite clears 1 Fatigue.
  • A good camp clears 3 Fatigue.
  • Each uninterrupted day in a proper safe haven clears 5 Fatigue.
  • Engaging in your Comfort during camp or downtime clears 1 additional Fatigue.
  • Recovery never lowers Fatigue below the current floor.

Interruptions, danger, deprivation, and strenuous activity may prevent recovery through the ordinary fiction.

Equipment and Resources

Slots and Open Pack

  • Most significant items and supply bundles occupy one slot.
  • Bulky equipment occupies two slots.
  • Objects occupying three or more slots are cumbersome cargo.
  • Pocket-sized possessions consume no slot until their quantity becomes meaningful.

Every character begins with a backpack that grants +3 slots. Other adventuring equipment may grant additional slots. Hirelings, beasts of burden, and carts can carry equipment beyond the character's personal capacity. What they can carry follows the physical fiction.

An Open Pack permission may reveal one ordinary, plausible tool the character could have packed. It cannot reveal survival resources, weapons, armor, treasure capacity, specialized tools, rare items, or magic. It refreshes only after proper resupply.

Resources and Protection

One ration feeds one character for one day. Track water only when scarcity matters. Mark every arrow or bolt fired. Torches burn in real time. An exhausted bundle frees its slot.

When armor or a shield could physically intercept a Wound, mark one of its occupied slots damaged instead. Protection works while an occupied slot remains unmarked. When all are marked, it is broken until repaired.

Travel and Exploration

Use a point crawl. Each route states its known or estimated duration, terrain, apparent danger as a direct d10 chance such as 2-in-10, and any specific landmarks, requirements, obstacles, or secrets.

  1. State the route's duration, terrain, and apparent danger.
  2. Players choose a route and describe preparations and precautions.
  3. For each consequential day, establish weather, roll route danger, and resolve the resulting fiction.
  4. If the party stops overnight, make camp.
  5. On arrival, return to ordinary freeform play.

The route decides whether danger produces an encounter, sign, discovery, weather shift, resource pressure, delay, or something else. If travel pressure does not matter, summarize the journey and arrive.

Explore dungeons and ruins through ordinary freeform play. Make location-specific danger checks when time, noise, delay, resting, backtracking, or conspicuous action creates exposure.

Camp

An overnight camp consumes that day's one ration per character. Establish the site, shelter, food, warmth, security, watchkeeping, and precautions. A good camp clears 3 Fatigue.

Each character may take one camp activity:

  • Tend: treat Wounds or Conditions with appropriate capability and supplies.
  • Repair: restore damaged gear, armor, or equipment.
  • Provision: forage, hunt, gather water, or prepare food.
  • Prepare: scout, fortify, conceal, study maps, or ready the next stage.
  • Connect: engage in your Comfort or share companionship, clear 1 additional Fatigue, and establish fitting character change.
  • Work: pursue research, training, crafting, spell recovery, or another undertaking.

These are prompts, not action ratings. Roll only when the activity is uncertain and consequential.

Magic

Each spell is a learned capability with a fixed Tier from 1–5 and text defining effect, scale, reach, requirements, and fictional permission. A character may attempt a known spell only when their Invoke rating equals or exceeds its Tier.

Casting uses the full Invoke pool and the same result table as every other Action. Tier does not remove dice or change the result bands. A relevant Feature may replace one Invoke d6 under the ordinary rule, but casting never requires a magical Feature.

Before rolling, gain Fatigue equal to the spell's Tier. This cost is paid regardless of the result and stacks with Fatigue from failure, Push, consequences, or other sources.

  • Failure: the spell fails and becomes Exhausted. Apply the ordinary fictional consequence.
  • Mixed success: the spell works and an ordinary fiction-driven consequence or cost lands. It causes no automatic additional Fatigue or exhaustion.
  • Clean success: the spell works as intended.
  • Exceptional success: the spell works with greater effect appropriate to its permission and scale.

A casting triggers one magical Mishap if it fails and at least one character die shows a natural 1, or if the casting's Fatigue causes the caster to reach 10. Resolve the spell and its ordinary result first. A threshold Mishap replaces the ordinary Fallout from that threshold; never apply both, and never trigger more than one Mishap from a casting.

One Work activity at camp restores all exhausted spells when the caster performs their tradition's recovery practice. Learned spells are lost only through explicit fiction that severs their source.

Mishaps should come from the spell entry or a short tradition table. Tier informs their possible severity and scale. A Mishap does not automatically exhaust a successfully cast spell unless its authored result says otherwise.

Downtime

After returning to a proper safe haven, each character may perform two downtime activities:

  • Seek Care: treat or heal a Wound, remove a Condition, or obtain specialized help.
  • Spend Wealth: convert money into XP through Revel, Devote, or Pursue and create fitting aftermath.
  • Undertake: pursue research, training, crafting, haven improvements, relationships, intelligence, faction influence, rare equipment, personal goals, crime, rumor, blackmail, fencing, or laying low.

Large projects may require several Undertakings. The activity limit measures consequential attention, not literal hours.

Conversation, relationship scenes, shopping, resupply, Open Pack refresh, and supported XP spending consume no activity. Fatigue clears 5 per uninterrupted safe-haven day down to its floor.

Use Shadowdark's carousing expenditure and XP scale for Spend Wealth. Revel, Devote, and Pursue provide equal XP; the choice changes the event and aftermath. Havens and bastions improve through Undertake and grant concrete permissions, capacities, and vulnerabilities.

Advancement

Treasure and consequential discoveries grant Shadowdark-compatible XP when returned to safety. Every participating character receives the full XP value.

End of Session

Each party question answered yes grants every participating character 1 XP:

  • Did we reach somewhere new or learn something consequential about the wider world or its history?
  • Did we survive or overcome a dangerous threat through violent or nonviolent means?
  • Did we leave a person, place, or community better off at meaningful cost?

Each character question answered yes grants that character 1 XP:

  • Did you reinforce, struggle against, or meaningfully change who your character is when it mattered?
  • Did your relationship with or opinion of someone or somewhere change through fellowship, conflict, comfort, or recovery?

Spend XP

Spend XP only during a quiet stretch in a safe haven and only when play supports the growth.

Deferred During This Playtest

  • A universal magical mishap table.
  • Dedicated Spend Wealth aftermath tables.
  • A universal haven or bastion upgrade catalog.

When a deferred trigger occurs, resolve it through the established fiction, record what the table needed, and use that evidence in the next design pass. Do not add a replacement subsystem during play.
