Eldspire Expedition Rules

How To Play

A fantasy adventure game about dangerous roads, hungry ruins, tired heroes, and the warmth that makes returning worth the risk.

Core Principles

  • Begin with the imagined world.
  • Players state what they want and how they pursue it.
  • Clarify what is possible, what is dangerous, and what could happen.
  • Roll only when the outcome is both uncertain and consequential.
  • Consequences follow from the established fiction.
  • The same procedure resolves every uncertain, consequential action.

The character sheet records evidence about who a character is and how they can affect the world.

Create a Character

Action Ratings

Actions describe practiced competence in concrete activities. They are rated from 0–5 and supply that many d6s when rolled.

ActionUsed to...
ExertLift, drag, break, hold, dig, or overpower an obstacle.
TraverseClimb, swim, leap, balance, chase, or escape through dangerous terrain.
SneakHide, infiltrate, steal unnoticed, or conceal something.
FightStrike, grapple, defend, or control someone in close combat.
ShootAttack accurately with bows, thrown weapons, or other ranged weapons.
SurveyActively search, reconnoiter, keep watch, or assess an immediate situation.
HuntTrack, stalk, forage, trap, or harvest creatures.
CraftBuild, repair, sabotage, disarm, or operate mechanisms and relics.
StudyRecall lore, decipher writing, investigate evidence, or understand something unfamiliar.
SwayPersuade, deceive, bargain, or provoke someone.
CommandLead, order, intimidate, or steady the frightened; handle or drive animals.
InvokeCast, control, bind, ward, or dispel magic.

Assign seven starting Action points: rate two Actions 2 and three Actions 1. Every other Action is untrained and rated 0.

Resistance

Three resistance ratings measure how well a character withstands what happens to them. Roll them in order, 1d6 each: 1 is 0, 2–3 is 1, 4–5 is 2, and 6 is 3. They are not chosen or bought and are separate from Action points.

RatingResists...
BodyInjury, poison, cold, and exhaustion.
MindConfusion, deception, and fear of the unknown.
SpiritDread, despair, magic, and temptation.

Background, Archetype, and Traits

Choose or generate one Background, one Archetype, and four Traits. Any Background may combine with any Archetype. A Background describes the community or circumstances that formed the character. An Archetype describes the kind of adventurer they are. Traits describe specific capabilities, experiences, reputations, and qualities.

Backgrounds, Archetypes, and Traits are unrated. Actions determine the base dice pool. When one Trait clearly applies, replace one d6 in the roll with a d10. Invoke at most one Trait per roll. These character facts also establish knowledge, access, permission, obligations, effect, consequences, and what the character notices.

Starting State

  • Fatigue 0.
  • Wounds 0, Conditions 0, and XP 0.
  • Body, Mind, and Spirit rolled as above.
  • Seven personal equipment slots plus a backpack granting three additional slots. The backpack's slots begin as Open Pack permissions.
  • A character whose Background, Archetype, or Traits grant magical permission begins with three Tier 1 spells total.

Resolve Uncertain Action

  1. Establish the immediate fiction.
  2. The player states a goal and method.
  3. Clarify what is possible, what threatens the attempt, and the likely consequences.
  4. If the outcome is uncertain and consequential, choose the Action matching the concrete act.
  5. Roll, apply the result, and change the fiction.

The approach decides the Action. No single Action owns locks, traps, pockets, or sleight of hand: Sneak picks a lock quietly, Exert forces it, Craft takes the mechanism apart, and Study puzzles out a trap.

Start with a number of d6s equal to the Action rating, then add any dice from Help or Push. If a Trait applies, replace one d6 with a d10. If the final pool contains at least one die, roll it and read the highest die.

If the final pool contains no dice, roll 2d6 and keep the lower die, or roll 2d10 and keep the lower die when a Trait applies. Only the kept die counts, and the result is capped at Clean success. A character with Action 0 rolls normally when Help or Push supplies at least one die.

DiceResult
Highest is 1–3Failure. The action fails and the established consequence lands.
Highest is 4–5Mixed success. The action succeeds and an established consequence or cost lands.
Highest is 6+Clean success. The action succeeds and avoids the immediate consequence.
Two or more dice show 6+Exceptional success. The action succeeds with greater effect or another fitting benefit.

Help

When characters commit to one coordinated action, state one shared goal and choose a lead. Each helper must contribute concretely in the fiction, gain 1 Fatigue, and add one d6 to the lead's pool. Roll once. Everyone placed in danger shares exposure to the announced consequences.

Push

Before rolling, gain 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s. The Fatigue is gained regardless of the result, and any other Fatigue stacks afterward. Fictional permission governs what a Push can accomplish.

Resist

When a consequence lands on your character, you may resist it with the rating that fits: Body, Mind, or Spirit. Roll that many d6s, or 2d6 and keep the lower at 0. Traits, Help, and Push do not add to the roll. Resisting reduces or avoids the consequence as the fiction supports, then costs Fatigue equal to 6 minus the highest die. If two or more dice show 6, it costs nothing and clears 1 Fatigue. Fatigue from resisting can trigger Fallout as usual.

How Actions and Threats Work

Conflict normally begins with what a character wants to change and how they act. Enemies, hazards, and other opposition obstruct that goal and threaten consequences.

State the goal and method, clarify the opposition and likely consequences, then roll when the exchange is uncertain and consequential. One roll resolves both the character's action and the opposition involved.

Read the result against the character's intent:

  • On a Clean or Exceptional success, the character achieves their intent and overcomes the immediate opposition.
  • On a Mixed success, the character achieves their intent and an established danger or related cost lands.
  • On a Failure, the threatened consequence lands or the opposition gains ground.

Example

Mara wants to cut off the pursuing ogre by collapsing a cracked arch. She can reach the weakened support, but the ogre will be on her before she finishes; a consequence could leave her trapped with it. One roll resolves both Mara's plan and the ogre's advance.

  • Exceptional success: the arch buries the ogre and leaves a safe route onward.
  • Clean success: the arch falls between the party and the ogre.
  • Mixed success: the arch blocks the ogre, but Mara is stranded on its side of the collapse.
  • Failure: the ogre reaches Mara first and sweeps her away from the support.

The normal rhythm: goal → opposition and stakes → one roll → changed fiction.

Fatigue, Wounds, and Conditions

Fatigue and Fallout

Fatigue is a visible clock from 0–10 measuring exertion, hunger, fear, weather, poor sleep, glancing harm, blood loss, magic, and deprivation.

  • Push: gain 1–3 Fatigue to add the same number of d6s.
  • Help: a helper who contributes a die gains 1 Fatigue.
  • Failure: after a consequential roll fails, the acting character gains 1 Fatigue.
  • Fictional pressure: established exertion, exposure, fear, violence, deprivation, or similar pressure can inflict 1–3 Fatigue: meaningful, severe, or overwhelming.
  • Explicit costs: spells, techniques, items, and other rules may list Fatigue as their price.

These sources stack. Clarify voluntary costs and threatened Fatigue before commitment when possible. Separate effects may add their own Fatigue after a Failure.

When your Fatigue reaches or exceeds 10, finish resolving the triggering action and its ordinary consequences. Then take Fallout: suffer one Wound shaped by the pressure that filled the clock, such as Frostbitten Toes, Torn Shoulder, or Shattered Nerves. A cause-specific Fallout table may determine the form it takes.

After Fallout, clear 10 Fatigue while retaining any excess.

Wounds

Wounds are lasting injuries caused by Wound-capable fiction or by Fallout, such as Broken Ribs, Concussion, Burned Hand, Gashed Thigh, or Crushed Knee. Name each one concretely.

Treating a Wound stabilizes it and changes its restrictions. Healing removes a Wound and follows the injury's fiction.

A Wound can be avoided or redirected only through concrete protection such as armor, a shield, sacrificed equipment, an ally, magic, retreat, or established positioning.

Conditions

Conditions are unranked fictional states such as Poisoned, Terrified, Lost, Hunted, Inspired, Disguised, Trusted by the Watch, or Blessed Against Fire. They directly change permission, position, and consequences. A Condition clears when its fiction is resolved.

Meet Death

When a character with three Wounds would take another, they become Dying and Meet Death. Describe how Death and her threshold appear, then roll 1d10.

  • 10: Death returns you but permanently marks you. Retain three Wounds.
  • 6-9: Death returns you Maimed, indebted, transformed, bound, or permanently changed. Leave the immediate scene.
  • 1-5: Your time has come. Choose a final exceptional deed followed by death, extraordinary supernatural transformation or servitude, or willing passage into death.

Maimed

Meeting Death can leave a character Maimed. Record the permanent injury or loss concretely, such as Lost Eye, Severed Hand, Ruined Leg, Destroyed Voice, or Body Withered by Death. A Maim changes fictional permissions, position, and consequences. Adaptation, prosthetics, or powerful magic may change its effects while preserving what happened. A character retires when a Maim makes continued adventuring impossible.

Recovery

Recovery requires safety, shelter, food, warmth, time, and appropriate care.

  • A meaningful moment of respite clears 1 Fatigue.
  • Each uninterrupted day in a proper safe haven clears 5 Fatigue.
  • An overnight camp clears 1–3 Fatigue, as described under Camp.
  • Engaging in your Comfort during downtime clears 1 Fatigue.

Interruptions, danger, deprivation, and strenuous activity may prevent recovery through the ordinary fiction.

Equipment and Resources

Slots and Open Pack

  • Most significant items and supply bundles occupy one slot.
  • Bulky equipment occupies two slots.
  • Objects occupying three or more slots are cumbersome cargo.
  • Pocket-sized possessions are zero-slot until their quantity becomes meaningful.

Every character begins with a backpack that grants +3 slots. Other adventuring equipment may grant additional slots. Hirelings, beasts of burden, and carts can carry equipment beyond the character's personal capacity. What they can carry follows the physical fiction.

An Open Pack permission may reveal one ordinary, plausible tool the character could have packed. Survival resources, weapons, armor, treasure capacity, specialized tools, rare items, and magic must already be carried. Open Pack refreshes after proper resupply.

Resources and Protection

One ration feeds one character for one day. Track water only when scarcity matters. Mark every arrow or bolt fired. Torches burn in real time. An exhausted bundle frees its slot.

When armor or a shield could physically intercept a Wound, mark one of its occupied slots damaged instead. Protection works while an occupied slot remains unmarked. When all are marked, it is broken until repaired in a haven.

Individual marks can be repaired at camp with the right kit. Each damaged mark has a 4-segment repair clock; see Repair under Camp.

Travel and Exploration

Use a point crawl. Each route states its length in days and any specific landmarks, requirements, obstacles, or secrets. Use the journey procedure when travel pressure matters; summarize safe or uneventful journeys.

Journey

Name the route's length in days, and the party chooses a guide. Then, for each travel day:

  1. Journey roll. The guide rolls to see how the day goes.
  2. Event check. Roll a d6. On a 1–2, an event happens.
  3. Event roll. If an event happens, roll the event dice openly and read the ladder.
  4. Play it out. Frame the event and handle it with ordinary Action rolls.
  5. Camp.

When the party has travelled the route's length in days, they arrive.

Journey Roll

Start with dice equal to the guide's rating in whichever Action fits how they lead that day: Traverse to push through the terrain, Survey to read the land and pick the route, Command to drive the column and its animals, or another Action the fiction supports. Then add or remove one die for each question:

Question+1d−1d
Do you know the way?A map, having been there before, or a road.Unknown or trackless land.
Are you ready?The right gear, full supplies, or travelling light.Heavy loads, wounded companions, or short on food.
Is the weather with you?Fair skies or a kind season.Storm, snow, or deep winter.
Is anyone helping you on the road?Friendly locals, a patron, or waystations.Hunted, followed, or racing a deadline.
Anything else?Another clear advantage.Another clear hardship.

Add +1d if someone Prepared at last night's camp. The pool holds at most 6 dice. A fitting Trait replaces one d6 with a d10 as usual. At 0 dice, roll 2d6 and keep the lower. Help and Push work as usual: another character who helps the guide gains 1 Fatigue and adds a d6, and the guide may Push for 1–3 Fatigue. The questions cover help from outside the party, such as locals, patrons, or waystations; Help is a companion's own effort. The 6-die limit applies to the whole pool.

ResultThe day
FailureA hard day. Each traveller gains 2 Fatigue.
Mixed successA tiring day. Each traveller gains 1 Fatigue.
Clean successA good day, with no cost.
Exceptional successA good day. Tonight each character takes two different camp activities.

Event Roll

When the event check calls for an event, start with 1d, then:

  • +1d if today's journey roll was a Clean or Exceptional success; −1d if it failed.
  • +1d if someone Scouted ahead at last night's camp and earned the bonus.
  • ±1d for anything else the fiction supports.

At 0 dice, roll 2d6 and keep the lower. Read the highest die:

ResultEvent
1Disaster. Two costs, and a Wound is possible.
2Hardship. One cost: something lost, or the trail.
3Obstacle. Go around for 1 day, or push through with a roll.
4Encounter. Someone or something; it could go either way.
5Sign. A sight, a clue, or news.
6Find. Shelter (a good camp tonight), a cache, or a ruin to explore.
Two 6sWonder. Everyone clears 1 Fatigue, and it plants a hook.

Pick or roll the specific event from the route and its terrain.

Play It Out

Frame the event as a situation with at least two ways through. Whoever acts rolls the Action that fits, and others may Help. A Clean success avoids the event's costs, a Mixed success pays one, and a Failure pays all of them. A cost is one of: 1 Fatigue for each traveller, a ration, a day, a piece of gear, or a Wound (Disaster only).

Hurry

The party may hurry through a day. A hurried day always costs each traveller 2 Fatigue, which replaces the journey roll's usual Fatigue, and that night nobody takes a camp activity or clears Fatigue. Its event check triggers on 1–3 instead of 1–2. The journey roll decides the pace:

ResultHurried day
Clean or Exceptional successThe party gains an extra day: take one more day off the journey. An Exceptional grants no extra camp activity, since there is no camp.
Mixed successThe party gains the extra day, but picks one: spend an extra ration, lose or break a piece of gear, or −1d on today's event roll.
FailureNo extra day. The party covered one day's ground and paid anyway.

Dungeons and Ruins

Explore dungeons and ruins through ordinary freeform play. Make location-specific danger checks when time, noise, delay, resting, backtracking, or conspicuous action creates exposure.

Camp

An overnight camp consumes that day's one ration per character. Establish the site, shelter, food, warmth, security, watchkeeping, and precautions.

Each character may take one camp activity, or two different activities after an Exceptional journey roll:

  • Repair: mend damaged armor, shields, or everyday gear. Requires a kit that fits the job, such as an armorer's kit for mail and plate or a leatherworking kit for leather and straps; without it, there is no Repair. Each damaged mark on armor or a shield has a 4-segment repair clock. Roll with the Action the pitch fits: a Failure fills 1 segment, a Mixed success 2, a Clean success 3, and two 6s clear the mark outright. When the clock fills, erase one damaged mark. Broken armor or shields can't be repaired on the road and need a haven. A snapped bowstring, torn tent, split strap, or cracked lantern takes one Repair with the right kit, with no clock.
  • Provision: forage, hunt, gather water, or prepare food. This is the only way to find food while travelling.
  • Prepare: study maps, plan the route, or ready the next stage. No roll; add +1d to tomorrow's journey roll.
  • Scout ahead: leave camp to range ahead of tomorrow's road. Roll with the Action the pitch fits. On a Clean or Exceptional success, add +1d to the next event roll. On a Mixed success, add +1d, but the scout gains 1 Fatigue. On a Failure, there is no bonus and the scout gains 1 Fatigue. It never affects the journey roll.
  • Connect: engage in your Comfort or share companionship, and establish fitting character change.
  • Work: pursue research, training, crafting, or another project, or recover spells. Spell recovery needs no roll. A project has a clock of 4, 6, or 8 segments, set by the fiction's scale; roll with the Action the pitch fits. A Failure fills 1 segment, a Mixed success 2, a Clean success 3, and two 6s complete the project.

Use these activities to frame what a character does in camp. Roll when the activity is uncertain and consequential.

Camp Recovery

At the end of camp, each character who ate a ration clears Fatigue:

NightFatigue cleared

Magic

Each spell is a learned capability with a fixed Tier from 1–5 and text defining effect, scale, reach, requirements, and fictional permission. A character may attempt a known spell only when their Invoke rating equals or exceeds its Tier.

Casting is an Invoke action and uses the ordinary dice resolution.

On a Mixed success or Failure, gain Fatigue equal to the spell's Tier. A Clean or Exceptional success costs no Fatigue. This cost stacks with Fatigue from failure, Push, consequences, or other sources.

  • Failure: the spell fails and becomes Exhausted. Gain Fatigue equal to its Tier and apply the ordinary fictional consequence.
  • Mixed success: the spell works. Gain Fatigue equal to its Tier, and an ordinary fiction-driven consequence or cost lands.
  • Clean success: the spell works as intended.
  • Exceptional success: the spell works with greater effect appropriate to its permission and scale.

A casting triggers a magical Mishap only when Fatigue gained from that casting causes the caster to reach or exceed 10. Resolve the spell and its ordinary result first. The Mishap replaces the ordinary Fallout from that threshold.

One Work activity at camp restores all exhausted spells when the caster performs their tradition's recovery practice. Learned spells remain known until explicit fiction severs their source.

Mishaps come from the spell entry or a short tradition table. Tier informs their possible severity and scale. An authored Mishap result states when it exhausts a successfully cast spell.

Downtime

After returning to a proper safe haven, each character may perform two downtime activities:

  • Seek Care: treat or heal a Wound, remove a Condition, or obtain specialized help.
  • Spend Wealth: convert money into XP through Revel, Devote, or Pursue and create fitting aftermath.
  • Undertake: pursue research, training, crafting, haven improvements, relationships, intelligence, faction influence, rare equipment, personal goals, crime, rumor, blackmail, fencing, or laying low.

Large projects may require several Undertakings. The activity limit measures consequential attention.

Conversation, relationship scenes, shopping, resupply, Open Pack refresh, and supported XP spending are free activities. Fatigue clears 5 per uninterrupted safe-haven day.

Use Shadowdark's carousing expenditure and XP scale for Spend Wealth. Revel, Devote, and Pursue provide equal XP; the choice changes the event and aftermath. Havens and bastions improve through Undertake and grant concrete permissions, capacities, and vulnerabilities.

Advancement

Treasure and consequential discoveries grant Shadowdark-compatible XP when returned to safety. Every participating character receives the full XP value.

End of Session

Each party question answered yes grants every participating character 1 XP:

  • Did we reach somewhere new or learn something consequential about the wider world or its history?
  • Did we survive or overcome a dangerous threat through violent or nonviolent means?
  • Did we leave a person, place, or community better off at meaningful cost?

Each character question answered yes grants that character 1 XP:

  • Did you reinforce, struggle against, or meaningfully change who your character is when it mattered?
  • Did your relationship with or opinion of someone or somewhere change through fellowship, conflict, comfort, or recovery?

Spend XP

Spend XP only during a quiet stretch in a safe haven and only when play supports the growth.
