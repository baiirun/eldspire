Eldspire Expedition Rules

How To Play

A fantasy adventure game about dangerous roads, hungry ruins, tired heroes, and the warmth that makes returning worth the risk.

Core Principles

  • Begin with the imagined world, not the character sheet.
  • Players state what they want and how they pursue it.
  • The GM describes what is possible, what is dangerous, and what could happen.
  • Roll only when the outcome is both uncertain and consequential.
  • Consequences follow from established fiction rather than a universal damage procedure.
  • The same procedure resolves travel, exploration, negotiation, violence, magic, and escape.

The character sheet records evidence about who a character is. It is not a menu limiting what they may attempt.

Character Texture

Define or generate a Background, inciting incident or motivation, Hearth Tie, Road Tie, Appearance, Keepsake, Mundane Item, Comfort, and Haven.

Texture is unrated. It establishes knowledge, access, permission, obligations, recovery, revelations, and consequences, but never adds dice. Repeatedly important texture may later become a Trait.

Starting State

  • Fatigue 0 and Fatigue Floor 0.
  • No Wounds, Conditions, or XP.
  • Seven personal equipment slots plus a backpack granting three additional slots. The backpack's slots begin as Open Pack permissions.
  • A character with a magical Feature begins with three Tier 1 spells total.

Resolve Uncertain Action

  1. Establish the immediate fiction.
  2. The player states a goal and method.
  3. The GM states what is possible, what threatens the attempt, and likely consequences.
  4. If the outcome is uncertain and consequential, choose one Feature and any situational Fatigue dice.
  5. Roll, apply the result, change the fiction, and move the spotlight.

Roll d10s equal to the chosen Feature's rating and keep the highest action die. If no Feature grounds the attempt, roll 2d10 and keep the lower die.

Highest Action DieResult
1-5Failure. Danger lands or the situation worsens.
6-9Mixed success. Achieve the intent with a consequence.
10Clean success. Achieve the intent and avoid the immediate threat.
Two or more 10sExceptional success. Exceed the original intent.

Separate Fatigue Dice

The GM assigns 0-3 Fatigue dice when the situation itself carries pressure. Roll them alongside the action pool but keep them separate. They never determine whether the action succeeds.

DicePressure
0Ordinary uncertainty.
1Meaningful exertion, exposure, fear, or instability.
2Severe pressure, serious injury, hostile environment, or desperate circumstances.
3Overwhelming danger or extreme exposure.
  • Each Fatigue die that rolls 1-5 adds 1 Fatigue.
  • Any Fatigue die that rolls 1 triggers one Rally check after resolving the action.
  • Never make more than one Rally check from a roll, including when that roll also raises Fatigue to 20.

Tag Team

When two or more characters commit to one coordinated action, describe one shared goal and how every participant contributes. Choose one participant's relevant Feature to form the action pool. Each additional credible participant adds one action die, regardless of their Feature rating. Roll once and resolve one shared outcome.

Merely declaring assistance does not qualify. Everyone who contributes a die acts concretely in the fiction, is exposed to the result, and cannot withdraw after seeing it.

Resolve the action's separate Fatigue dice once. Each Fatigue die showing 1-5 adds 1 Fatigue to one participant according to the fiction. If several are equally exposed, the group chooses who bears it. A 1 triggers one Rally check by the participant who bears that Fatigue; if several 1s affect different participants, choose one of them to Rally. Never make more than one Rally check from the action.

The action produces one shared consequence. It may affect one, several, or every participant according to the established threat. Do not automatically copy every Fatigue or consequence onto everyone.

Push

Before rolling, you may Push once by invoking one additional applicable Trait. Describe how that Trait drives the character beyond their ordinary approach. Add one ordinary action die, regardless of the Trait's rating, and add one ordinary Fatigue die to the action's existing Fatigue pool.

The additional dice follow their normal rules and do not need to remain distinguishable. The invoked Trait must apply distinctly rather than restating the primary Feature. An Archetype cannot be invoked as the additional Push Trait. Push cannot make an impossible action possible or bypass a fictional restriction without a credible fictional answer.

During Tag Team, any participant may invoke one of their Traits to Push, but the shared action can still be Pushed only once. Resolve the added Fatigue die through the ordinary shared-exposure rule.

Position and Consequences

Position has no rated categories. Communicate it concretely: the ledge is crumbling, the crossbow is trained on you, the passage is too narrow for the axe, or the ritual will attract something.

Consequences can add Fatigue, inflict a Wound or Condition, damage or consume equipment, remove opportunity, cause delay or exposure, change a relationship, or let an announced threat land. An established consequence does not require another roll.

Spotlight and Opposition

There is no separate combat mode and enemies do not roll. The GM announces threats; players describe how they answer. Move the spotlight after every resolved action so every positioned character can intervene. Use clockwise order only if free spotlight becomes chaotic.

Ordinary opposition may fall after one decisive action. Durable threats may require fictional breakpoints, vulnerabilities, or a short progress track.

Enemies and Hazards

Build people, monsters, traps, weather, and supernatural hazards from the fiction outward. Their descriptions establish what they can do, what can affect them, and what consequences they can inflict. They do not need conventional combat statistics.

  1. State what it is: establish its nature, behavior, sensory presence, and enough history to place it in the world.
  2. Give it pressure: decide what it wants now and how the situation changes if the characters hesitate.
  3. Find its defining truth: state the one fictional quality players must respect.
  4. Write two to four moves: concrete warnings and consequences that change the situation.
  5. Add counterplay only when needed: provide leverage for threats that are immune, recurring, or otherwise unreadable.
  6. Add endurance only when needed: use visible breakpoints or a short track when several meaningful changes in position should be required.

Threat Format

Name

One or two evocative sentences establishing nature, behavior, pressure, and important fictional qualities.

Two to four concrete ways it advances the danger.

Optional counterplay, escalation, endurance, or special procedure.

Most ordinary people and minor threats fall, flee, surrender, or change after one decisive action. Endurance represents visible fictional change rather than hidden hit points. Use the Threat Spark Tables when a premise does not arrive immediately.

Fatigue, Wounds, and Conditions

Fatigue and Rally

Fatigue is the toll of exertion, hunger, fear, weather, poor sleep, glancing harm, blood loss, magic, and deprivation. It ranges from 0-20 and can never recover below the current Fatigue Floor. The fiction may add Fatigue directly.

At 20 Fatigue, Rally and stop. The character cannot continue until recovery lowers Fatigue below 20.

To Rally, roll 1d20. Roll over current Fatigue to keep control. Roll equal to or under it to use the failed result below.

d20Fallout
1-5Fray. Suffer one immediate fallout: deplete a relevant resource, damage relevant equipment, lose position, or gain a fitting short-lived Condition.
6-10Struggle. Suffer one lasting fallout: lose or break equipment, lose a resource bundle or treasure, gain a Condition requiring meaningful recovery, or let an established threat land.
11-15Break. Suffer one severe fallout: a Wound when supported by the established danger, a Condition that removes you from the scene, or the loss of something important to the expedition.
16-19Collapse. Suffer one expedition-ending fallout: a Wound or Condition whose established fiction prevents continuing, or catastrophic equipment, resource, route, or objective loss.
20Taken Out. The established pressure removes you from the expedition. Returning requires rescue, substantial recovery, or meaningful transformation.

Resolve the triggering action first. Rally never changes that action's result or undoes achieved intent; it changes what follows. The GM selects only fallout supported by the established pressure. When several costs are equally credible, offer two and let the player choose. One result creates one coherent fallout rather than a checklist of penalties.

Rally never clears Fatigue. At 20 Fatigue, stop after Rally regardless of the result. Rally can inflict a Wound only when the established fiction is Wound-capable, and death remains governed by Wounds and Meet Death.

Hard mode: 2d10 Rally

For a harsher Fatigue spiral, roll 2d10 instead of 1d20 and compare the total to current Fatigue normally. Low Fatigue is easier to withstand, but Rally becomes sharply less reliable above 10 Fatigue; use the total on the same fallout table. The lowest possible result is 2.

Treating a Wound stabilizes it and changes its restrictions. Use the treated floor only when every current Wound is treated. Healing removes a Wound and follows the injury's fiction.

A Wound can be avoided or redirected only through concrete protection such as armor, a shield, sacrificed equipment, an ally, magic, retreat, or established positioning.

Conditions

Conditions are unranked fictional states such as Poisoned, Terrified, Lost, Cursed, or Weary. They change permission, position, and consequences rather than applying standard penalties. A Condition clears when its fiction is resolved.

Meet Death

When a character with three Wounds would take another, they become Dying and Meet Death. Describe how Death and her threshold appear, then roll 1d10.

  • 10: Death returns you but permanently marks you. Retain three Wounds.
  • 6-9: Death returns you Maimed, indebted, transformed, bound, or permanently changed. Leave the immediate scene.
  • 1-5: Your time has come. Choose a final exceptional deed followed by death, extraordinary supernatural transformation or servitude, or willing passage into death.

Recovery

Recovery requires safety, shelter, food, warmth, time, and appropriate care.

  • A meaningful moment of respite clears 1 Fatigue.
  • A good camp clears 3 Fatigue.
  • Each uninterrupted day in a proper safe haven clears 5 Fatigue.
  • Recovery never lowers Fatigue below the current floor.

Interruptions, danger, deprivation, and strenuous activity may prevent recovery through the ordinary fiction.

Equipment and Resources

Slots and Open Pack

  • Most significant items and supply bundles occupy one slot.
  • Bulky equipment occupies two slots.
  • Objects occupying three or more slots are cumbersome cargo.
  • Pocket-sized possessions consume no slot until their quantity becomes meaningful.

Every character begins with a backpack that grants +3 slots. Other adventuring equipment may grant additional slots. Hirelings, beasts of burden, and carts can carry equipment beyond the character's personal capacity. What they can carry follows the physical fiction.

An Open Pack permission may reveal one ordinary, plausible tool the character could have packed. It cannot reveal survival resources, weapons, armor, treasure capacity, specialized tools, rare items, or magic. It refreshes only after proper resupply.

Resources and Protection

One ration feeds one character for one day. Track water only when scarcity matters. Mark every arrow or bolt fired. Torches burn in real time. An exhausted bundle frees its slot.

When armor or a shield could physically intercept a Wound, mark one of its occupied slots damaged instead. Protection works while an occupied slot remains unmarked. When all are marked, it is broken until repaired.

Travel and Exploration

Use a point crawl. Each route states its known or estimated duration, terrain, apparent danger as a direct d10 chance such as 2-in-10, and any specific landmarks, requirements, obstacles, or secrets.

  1. State the route's duration, terrain, and apparent danger.
  2. Players choose a route and describe preparations and precautions.
  3. For each consequential day, establish weather, roll route danger, and resolve the resulting fiction.
  4. If the party stops overnight, make camp.
  5. On arrival, return to ordinary freeform play.

The route decides whether danger produces an encounter, sign, discovery, weather shift, resource pressure, delay, or something else. If travel pressure does not matter, summarize the journey and arrive.

Explore dungeons and ruins through ordinary freeform play. Make location-specific danger checks when time, noise, delay, resting, backtracking, or conspicuous action creates exposure.

Camp

An overnight camp consumes that day's one ration per character. Establish the site, shelter, food, warmth, security, watchkeeping, and precautions. A good camp clears 3 Fatigue.

Each character may take one camp activity:

  • Tend: treat Wounds or Conditions with appropriate capability and supplies.
  • Repair: restore damaged gear, armor, or equipment.
  • Provision: forage, hunt, gather water, or prepare food.
  • Prepare: scout, fortify, conceal, study maps, or ready the next stage.
  • Connect: share comfort or companionship, clear 1 additional Fatigue, and establish fitting character change.
  • Work: pursue research, training, crafting, spell recovery, or another undertaking.

These are prompts, not action ratings. Roll only when the activity is uncertain and consequential.

Magic

Each spell has a fixed Tier from 1-5 and text defining effect, scale, reach, requirements, and permission. To cast it, know the spell and use a relevant magical Feature rated at least as high as its Tier.

  1. Build the action pool from the magical Feature.
  2. Convert action dice equal to the spell's Tier into Arcane dice.
  3. Roll normally. Arcane dice remain action dice.
  4. Read Arcane dice for Fatigue, exhaustion, and mishaps.
  • Each Arcane die showing 1-5 adds 1 Fatigue.
  • Any Arcane die showing 1 exhausts the spell after the attempt.
  • Two or more Arcane dice showing 1 trigger one magical mishap.
  • Arcane dice never trigger Rally.

One Work activity at camp restores all exhausted spells when the caster performs their tradition's recovery practice. Learned spells are lost only through explicit fiction that severs their source.

Until a mishap table exists, resolve a trigger as a severe magical consequence shaped by the spell, Tier, tradition, place, and immediate fiction. Record it for later design.

Downtime

After returning to a proper safe haven, each character may perform two downtime activities:

  • Seek Care: treat or heal a Wound, remove a Condition, or obtain specialized help.
  • Spend Wealth: convert money into XP through Revel, Devote, or Pursue and create fitting aftermath.
  • Undertake: pursue research, training, crafting, haven improvements, relationships, intelligence, faction influence, rare equipment, personal goals, crime, rumor, blackmail, fencing, or laying low.

Large projects may require several Undertakings. The activity limit measures consequential attention, not literal hours.

Conversation, relationship scenes, shopping, resupply, Open Pack refresh, and supported XP spending consume no activity. Fatigue clears 5 per uninterrupted safe-haven day down to its floor.

Use Shadowdark's carousing expenditure and XP scale for Spend Wealth. Revel, Devote, and Pursue provide equal XP; the choice changes the event and aftermath. Havens and bastions improve through Undertake and grant concrete permissions, capacities, and vulnerabilities.

Advancement

Treasure and consequential discoveries grant Shadowdark-compatible XP when returned to safety. Every participating character receives the full XP value.

End of Session

Each party question answered yes grants every participating character 1 XP:

  • Did we reach somewhere new or learn something consequential about the wider world or its history?
  • Did we survive or overcome a dangerous threat through violent or nonviolent means?
  • Did we leave a person, place, or community better off at meaningful cost?

Each character question answered yes grants that character 1 XP:

  • Did you reinforce, struggle against, or meaningfully change who your character is when it mattered?
  • Did your relationship with or opinion of someone or somewhere change through fellowship, conflict, comfort, or recovery?

Spend XP

Spend XP only during a quiet stretch in a safe haven and only when play supports the growth.

Deferred During This Playtest

  • A universal magical mishap table.
  • Dedicated Spend Wealth aftermath tables.
  • A universal haven or bastion upgrade catalog.

When a deferred trigger occurs, resolve it through the established fiction, record what the table needed, and use that evidence in the next design pass. Do not add a replacement subsystem during play.
